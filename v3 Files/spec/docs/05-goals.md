# Goals

## Two types

**Strategic** — why they're here at all. "Stop living paycheck to paycheck."
Set during onboarding. One at a time.

**Tactical** — concrete milestones. "Build a $3,000 emergency fund." Several
at once.

Both feed task prioritization on home. If a user sets no goals, a fallback
default applies rather than leaving the priority engine without an anchor.

## Goals mostly happen elsewhere

The Goals tab tracks progress. It is not where most goals are born.

After a meaningful action — finishing a budget, completing a lesson, submitting
a journal entry — the app offers one to three goal suggestions drawn from
whatever is on screen. The last option is always "create your own."

Suggestions are scoped to context. On the overall budget, they span categories.
Inside housing, they're housing-specific.

Once enough goals are in flight, the suggestion becomes an "update your goals"
button instead. Do not let a user accumulate goals they'll never track.

## Updating progress

Never ask someone to update a number directly. Ask something they actually
know:

- "Roughly what's in your checking account?" → updates savings goals
- "How much did you spend on dining out this month?" → updates spend-limit goals

Rooted in lifestyle events, not in bookkeeping. The number moves as a
consequence of an answer, not as the answer itself.

## Display

Each goal shows target, current, and pace — ahead, on track, or behind. Pace
matters more than the raw figure: $620 of $3,000 means little; "41% of the pace
you set" means something.

The seeded emergency fund goal is deliberately behind (D17). It should feel
recoverable, not alarming.

**Done when:** goals can be created contextually and through the tab, progress
updates via event-based prompts, and pace renders for every goal.
